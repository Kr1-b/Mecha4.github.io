Waterproofing

The fish skin floods on purpose. Only the dry pod inside has to stay dry, and water has three ways into it.

  • 1The end capThe cap plugs into the tube with two 70 × 2.5 mm O-rings around its edge, squeezed about 20%. Water pressure pushes them harder into the gap, so this kind of seal gets tighter the deeper it goes. Four M3 screws just stop the cap sliding out.
  • 2Fin and rudder shaftsEach 3 mm shaft runs through a boss with two internal O-ring grooves. The shafts only swing back and forth, so greased O-rings hold well at pool depth.
  • 3Tail crank shaftThis one spins nonstop, which wears O-rings. Grease it well and check it after every test. If it starts to weep, switch to a magnetic coupling so no shaft passes through the cap at all.

O-ring squeeze check

An O-ring seals because it gets squashed. Too little and it leaks, too much and it tears or jams the servo. Try the two seals in this design, or your own numbers.

Will it float or sink?

A sealed tube full of air floats hard. You want the finished fish just barely positive, so it rises on its own if the battery dies. The defaults come from the CAD: the pod plus all the printed parts displace about 1,400 cm³ and weigh about 850 g printed solid in PETG. The electronics add about 185 g.

Printing for water

  • Print the pod and end cap in PETG or ASA, not PLA (PLA softens and absorbs water over time). The skin and fins flood anyway, so PETG is enough there.
  • Print the pod standing on its open end with at least 5 walls, and use 100% infill on the end cap and glands. Normal FDM walls leak through the layer lines otherwise.
  • Sand the inside of the tube mouth smooth where the end cap O-rings slide, then wipe on a thin film of silicone grease.
  • Coat the outside with a thin layer of epoxy (e.g. XTC-3D) to close the layer lines.
  • Ream the 3 mm shaft bores with a 3 mm drill bit after printing so the shafts turn freely.
  • A resin printer gives watertight walls and accurate O-ring grooves straight off the printer, if you can get one.

Leak test before electronics

  1. Assemble the pod, end cap, O-rings and shafts with no electronics inside. Put a dry tissue in the tube.
  2. Push the end cap in until it seats, then fit the four M3 screws.
  3. Weigh it down and hold it at the bottom of a bucket for 30 minutes, turning the shafts by hand.
  4. Open it and check the tissue. Any wet spot shows you which seal leaked.
  5. Fix that seal and repeat until the tissue stays dry, then fit the electronics with a silica gel pack.
