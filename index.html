Three motions, three parts

  • Forward: the tailA motor turns the crank, and the crank flaps the tail side to side. One crank turn is one tail beat.
  • Up and down: pectoral finsTwo servos tilt the side fins. Nose-up fins lift the fish as it swims, nose-down fins drive it deeper.
  • Left and right: the rudderA servo under the lid turns the rudder on top, yawing the fish toward the side it points.

Built to stay dry

The Arduino, servos, motor and battery all live in one sealed dry box inside the printed body. Only three thin shafts leave it, and each passes through two greased O-rings.

The simulator's physics are simplified, but the trends are right: thrust grows with the square of tail-beat frequency, and the fins only change depth while the fish is moving.
